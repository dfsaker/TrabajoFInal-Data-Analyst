Conclusions of the Productivity and Remote Work Analysis

1. Executive Summary (Overall Productivity)

The company’s overall average Productivity Index is 3.265.

The employee with the highest identified productivity is E0363 from the IT department, with an index of 6.033333. This is the result of a performance score of 91, a satisfaction level of 9, and working 30 hours per week.


2. Analysis of Key Questions

A. Does remote work improve or reduce performance?
Remote work does not significantly affect productivity.

The average performance of on-site workers (Non-Remote) is 3.233669.

The average performance of remote workers (Remote) is 3.284819.

The difference between the two averages is only 1.58%, a variation considered relatively small.

Conclusion: It can be inferred that the work modality (remote or on-site) is not the determining factor in productivity, suggesting that other factors (such as satisfaction or absences) carry more weight.

B. Which departments present the greatest opportunities for improvement?

The greatest opportunities for improvement are found in departments with the lowest average productivity index.

The Sales department has the lowest average index: 3.177441.

The Operations department has the second-lowest index: 3.220159.

Highest performing departments:

Marketing: 3.322139

Support: 3.300560

3. Additional Findings

Relationship Between Absences and Performance
The analysis shows an interesting (though non-linear) correlation between the number of absences and performance:

The highest average performance (3.613986) corresponds to employees with 1 absence.

Performance appears to drop as absences increase, reaching its lowest point at 5 absences (3.118840).

The performance of employees with zero absences (3.322539) remains above the general average.
